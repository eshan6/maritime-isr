## 0. Plain-English glossary for this document

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Precision** | Of the vessels we flagged, what fraction really were doing something odd. ADR-004 requires ≥0.7 for anything an analyst sees. |
| **Recall** | Of the vessels that really were odd, what fraction we flagged. |
| **Base rate** | What fraction of the fleet is odd to begin with. Flagging at random gets you precision equal to the base rate, so a number at or below it is worth nothing. |
| **Hull** | One physical ship. The unit everything is split and scored on. |
| **Fit / dev / test split** | Three separate groups of hulls. The model learns on *fit*, every choice and threshold is made on *dev*, and *test* is opened once at the very end so the number reported was not tuned on. |
| **Flow field / route prior** | A learned map of "traffic arriving in this patch of sea on this heading tends to go **that** way next, at **that** speed". |
| **Residual score** | "How far is she from where we predicted?" The signal ADR-042 measured, and rejected. |
| **Novelty / likelihood score** | "How improbable is what she is doing, given what traffic does here?" The signal this session builds. The difference matters: a waypoint turn is a large residual but is not improbable, because the model knows traffic turns there. |
| **Motion-expressed anomaly** | An injected anomaly a motion-only detector could in principle see (`dark_transfer`, `behavioural_geographic`, part of `spoofing`). A bad IMO check digit is not one. |

## 1. The situation on arrival

ADR-042 measured route-aware forward projection and reported precision of
**0.09–0.33 against a base rate of 0.15** — at or below chance — at every
operating point that flagged enough hulls to measure. The mechanism it named:

* the prediction improved at the **median** (−26% at 3 h, −43% at 6 h) and not
  at the **tail** (−6% and −1% at p90), and the cone is sized at p90; and
* "did she depart from her own dead-reckoned path" is a question nearly every
  vessel answers yes to, because every vessel alters course at every waypoint.
  It does not **select**.

### 1.1 The corpus had to be rebuilt first

The pipeline run this session inherited had **died mid-`scenario generate`**
(process gone; log last written 09:50, session resumed 16:49), leaving
`ais_position` at 304,571 rows and `scenario_truth` **empty**. That corpus
could not have measured anything. It was regenerated from the deterministic
seed-7 generator; the target state, from the last complete run, is:

* 674 vessels, **820,805** AIS positions, 96 truth rows
  (52 true anomalies / 38 decoys / 6 deliberate misses),
* `build-tracks` → 662 tracks.

That is ~3× the corpus ADR-042 fitted on (239 tracks / 238 hulls), which is the
first thing that makes a precision claim here worth stating at all: ADR-042's
own §7 flagged that rows with fewer than ~7 flagged hulls are noise.

Regenerated corpus, confirmed on disk: **820,878 AIS positions**, 52
`TRUE_ANOMALY` rows naming **62 hulls**, with families:

| family | hulls named | motion-expressed? |
|---|---|---|
| `behavioural_geographic` | 24 | **yes** |
| `dark_transfer` | 23 | **yes** |
| `identity_manipulation` | 11 | no |
| `graph_ownership` | 6 | no |
| `paperwork` | 4 | no |
| `spoofing` | 3 | partly |
| `imagery` | 2 | no |

### 1.2 The bar is higher than ADR-042's, not lower

ADR-042 measured against a base rate of **0.15** on 96 held-out hulls. The new
corpus is three times larger but the anomalies did not triple, so the base rate
over the whole fleet is **62/674 ≈ 0.092**. Reaching precision 0.70 from there
is a **lift of about 7.6×**, against the 2.3× the best ADR-042 operating point
managed. That is stated up front because it is the single most important number
for calibrating expectations, and because a reader who assumes the bar moved
down with the base rate would draw the opposite conclusion from any result here.

## 2. Protocol — fit / dev / test *(binding, written before any number)*

Stated here **before** the measurement so it cannot be adjusted afterwards.

* **Split by HULL, three ways**, never by track. A hull in two splits lets the
  model memorise her.
* **40 / 30 / 30**, seed 7, `prediction_eval.hull_split_3way`. Not the usual
  60/20/20: ADR-042 fitted a serviceable flow field on 142 hulls, so 40% of this
  corpus (~270) is already past where the field stops improving, while what
  ADR-042 lacked was scored hulls — its §7 has rows computed on one to six
  flagged hulls. Statistical power is the scarce thing here, so the split spends
  the surplus on dev and test.
* **fit** — the flow field, the baselines and any calibration are fitted here,
  and nowhere else.
* **dev** — every design choice, every feature kept or dropped, and every
  threshold is chosen here. Looked at as often as needed.
* **test** — opened **once**, at the very end, and the number it gives is the
  result. If it is touched more than once, this document says so explicitly and
  in the same sentence as the number.
* **Every precision figure carries the flagged-hull count.** A precision over
  fewer than ~10 flagged hulls is noise and is labelled as such (ADR-042 §7).
* **Two scoring targets, both reported**: against *all* injected anomalies (what
  the operator will ask about), and against **motion-expressed families only**
  (`dark_transfer`, `behavioural_geographic`, `spoofing`) — the honest target
  for a motion-only signal, since no motion detector can see a bad IMO check
  digit or a paperwork mismatch.
* **No detector code reads `scenario_truth`.** Only the measurement harness
  does, after the fact.
