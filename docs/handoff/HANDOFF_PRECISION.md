# HANDOFF — motion-signal precision

**Session:** raising motion/prediction-based anomaly precision toward ADR-004's
≥0.70, following ADR-042.
**Branch:** `claude/system-tech-overview-4w7amz`.
**Status:** COMPLETE. Result: **precision 0.70 was not reached and is not
reachable on this corpus by a motion-only signal**; the measured figure on the
held-out test split is **0.032**, the oracle ceiling is **0.20**, and the
recommendation is **not to promote**. The reasoning is in §2A; the mechanism is
in §4.1. An honest ceiling is the deliverable, as it was for ADR-042.

> **Every number in this document is on the synthetic corpus.** The corpus is
> generated by one deterministic route generator (`scenario/searoute.py`), so
> any route/flow model fitted to it is flattered by construction — ADR-042
> already records this as OQ-pred-2. Nothing here may be stated as a live
> figure (CLAUDE.md §4.6, §5). No dark vessel has been detected on real data.

## 0. Plain-English glossary for this document

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Precision** | Of the vessels we flagged, what fraction really were doing something odd. ADR-004 requires ≥0.7 for anything an analyst sees. |
| **Recall** | Of the vessels that really were odd, what fraction we flagged. |
| **Base rate** | What fraction of the fleet is odd to begin with. Flagging at random gets you precision equal to the base rate, so a number at or below it is worth nothing. |
| **Hull** | One physical ship. The unit everything is split and scored on. |
| **Fit / dev / test split** | Three separate groups of hulls. The model learns on *fit*, every choice and threshold is made on *dev*, and *test* is opened once at the very end so the number reported was not tuned on. |
| **Flow field / route prior** | A learned map of "traffic arriving in this patch of sea on this heading tends to go **that** way next, at **that** speed". |
| **Residual score** | "How far is she from where we predicted?" The signal ADR-042 measured, and rejected. |
| **Novelty / likelihood score** | "How improbable is what she is doing, given what traffic does here?" The signal this session builds. The difference matters: a waypoint turn is a large residual but is not improbable, because the model knows traffic turns there. |
| **Motion-expressed anomaly** | An injected anomaly a motion-only detector could in principle see (`dark_transfer`, `behavioural_geographic`, part of `spoofing`). A bad IMO check digit is not one. |

## 1. The situation on arrival

ADR-042 measured route-aware forward projection and reported precision of
**0.09–0.33 against a base rate of 0.15** — at or below chance — at every
operating point that flagged enough hulls to measure. The mechanism it named:

* the prediction improved at the **median** (−26% at 3 h, −43% at 6 h) and not
  at the **tail** (−6% and −1% at p90), and the cone is sized at p90; and
* "did she depart from her own dead-reckoned path" is a question nearly every
  vessel answers yes to, because every vessel alters course at every waypoint.
  It does not **select**.

The pipeline run this session inherited had **died mid-`scenario generate`**
(process gone; log last written 09:50, session resumed 16:49), leaving
`ais_position` at 304,571 rows and `scenario_truth` **empty**. That corpus
could not have measured anything. It was regenerated from the deterministic

* 674 vessels, **820,805** AIS positions, 96 truth rows
  (52 true anomalies / 38 decoys / 6 deliberate misses),
* `build-tracks` → 662 tracks.

That is ~3× the corpus ADR-042 fitted on (239 tracks / 238 hulls), which is the
first thing that makes a precision claim here worth stating at all: ADR-042's

Regenerated corpus, confirmed on disk: **820,878 AIS positions**, 52

|---|---|---|

### 1.2 The bar is higher than ADR-042's, not lower

ADR-042 measured against a base rate of **0.15** on 96 held-out hulls. The new
corpus is three times larger but the anomalies did not triple, so the base rate
over the whole fleet is **62/674 ≈ 0.092**. Reaching precision 0.70 from there
is a **lift of about 7.6×**, against the 2.3× the best ADR-042 operating point
managed. That is stated up front because it is the single most important number
for calibrating expectations, and because a reader who assumes the bar moved
down with the base rate would draw the opposite conclusion from any result here.

## 2. Protocol — fit / dev / test *(binding, written before any number)*

Stated here **before** the measurement so it cannot be adjusted afterwards.

* **Split by HULL, three ways**, never by track. A hull in two splits lets the
  model memorise her.
* **40 / 30 / 30**, seed 7, `prediction_eval.hull_split_3way`. Not the usual
  60/20/20: ADR-042 fitted a serviceable flow field on 142 hulls, so 40% of this
  corpus (~270) is already past where the field stops improving, while what
  ADR-042 lacked was scored hulls — its §7 has rows computed on one to six
  flagged hulls. Statistical power is the scarce thing here, so the split spends
  the surplus on dev and test.
* **fit** — the flow field, the baselines and any calibration are fitted here,
  and nowhere else.
* **dev** — every design choice, every feature kept or dropped, and every
  threshold is chosen here. Looked at as often as needed.
* **test** — opened **once**, at the very end, and the number it gives is the
  result. If it is touched more than once, this document says so explicitly and
  in the same sentence as the number.
* **Every precision figure carries the flagged-hull count.** A precision over
  fewer than ~10 flagged hulls is noise and is labelled as such (ADR-042 §7).
* **Two scoring targets, both reported**: against *all* injected anomalies (what
  the operator will ask about), and against **motion-expressed families only**
  (`dark_transfer`, `behavioural_geographic`, `spoofing`) — the honest target
  for a motion-only signal, since no motion detector can see a bad IMO check
  digit or a paperwork mismatch.
* **No detector code reads `scenario_truth`.** Only the measurement harness
  does, after the fact.

> **How this protocol was actually kept** (added after the fact, and the
> section above is left exactly as it was written so the commitment and the
> outcome can be read against each other): every clause held except the
> single-opening rule. Test was opened **twice** — once for the reported result
> (§5.2) and once, deliberately, for the oracle ceiling (§5.5). The clause above
> anticipated this and required the disclosure to travel with the number; it
> does, in §5.5 and §7.

## 2A. THE HEADLINE — read this if you read nothing else

**Precision ≥0.70 was not reached, and on this corpus it is not reachable by a
motion-only signal. The number on the held-out test split is 0.032** (31
windows flagged, 1 correct, 95% CI 0.006–0.162), against a base rate of 0.016 —
a lift of **2.0×**, statistically indistinguishable from chance.

That figure replaces an earlier one of **0.40 on dev**, and the gap between them
is the actual finding of this session. The 0.40 was selection noise: it was the
best of ~1,700 candidate rules evaluated against **16 positive windows**. It did
not survive contact with hulls the thresholds had not been chosen on.

Four things were established, in order:

1. **The per-hull measurement was measuring the generator, not the vessels.**
   Anomalous hulls in this corpus are watched for a median of **15 hours** and
   ordinary ones for **149**. Track length alone separates them with AUC 0.88 —
   better than any behavioural feature in the profile. Any per-hull precision
   figure from this corpus, including one that had reached 0.70, would have been
   worthless.
2. **Fixed-length windows remove it, demonstrably.** The positive rate is flat
   across window-span quartiles and across fix-count quartiles, on both fit+dev
   and test, with overlapping intervals (§4.1).
3. **With it removed, the signal is at chance on held-out hulls.** ADR-042's
   conclusion about forward projection extends to the novelty reformulation. The
   novelty score **should not be promoted** to a suspicion factor (§7).
4. **The ceiling is 0.20, and it is proven rather than asserted.** Granting a
   rule the answer key *at selection time* — thresholds chosen on the test
   split, ~4,100 candidate rules, two window lengths — the best precision
   reachable is **0.200** (§5.5). So 0.70 is not a tuning gap that more dev
   positives would close; it is out of reach for a motion-only signal on this
   corpus. That second opening of test is disclosed in §5.5 and §7.

An honest ceiling is the deliverable here, as ADR-042 was. The number was not
manufactured.

## 3. What was built

### 3.1 The window (`tracks/novelty.py`)

`Window`, `windows_of`, `hull_windows`, `Rule`, `Verdict`, `flag` — the names
`__all__` already promised and the module did not yet implement.

A **window** is a fixed 12-hour slice of one track, tiled from her first fix,
non-overlapping, kept only when fixes actually span ≥80% of the nominal length
(`MIN_WINDOW_SPAN_FRACTION`). That last clause is what makes "fixed length" true
rather than nominal: without it every track ends in a stub of arbitrary length,
the stubs are systematically shorter for briefly-watched hulls, and the confound
walks back in through the ragged edge.

Windowing also **reverses** the residual bias into the safe direction. A hull
watched for 500 hours contributes forty windows and therefore forty chances to
look odd; one watched for twelve contributes one. A signal that still separates
under that is separating on behaviour.

Per-fix surprises are computed once per track and bucketed, so windowing does
not multiply the cost of scoring and every window sees exactly the surprises the
whole-track profile saw.

### 3.2 Time-aware labels (`scenario/measure.hull_anomaly_intervals`)

`scenario_truth` carries `t_start`/`t_end`, which the per-hull key discarded.
Once the unit is a window this matters: a three-hour rendezvous inside a
three-hundred-hour track leaves her ordinary for the rest of it, and labelling
all her windows positive would score a correct silence as a miss *and* reward a
detector that fired on her constantly. `TruthIntervals.covers` labels by
overlap, not containment.

The reader stayed in `scenario/measure.py` — the one module allowed to name that
table — and the arithmetic in `tracks/prediction_eval.py`, which the isolation
check treats as detection code. `test_novelty_and_its_harness_never_read_the_answer_key`
still passes over both modules.

### 3.3 The confound check itself (`prediction_eval.span_flatness`)

Positive rate by quartile of any window quantity, with Wilson intervals. Run on
`observed_span_hours` to show the windowing worked, and on `n_fixes`,
`check_coverage`, `offshore_km_median` and `n_windows_in_hull` to hunt for a
replacement — because removing one confound and inheriting another is the same
mistake with a longer paper trail. It is unit-tested against a *planted*
confound, so a flat report is evidence rather than an absence of evidence.

### 3.4 One defect found and fixed mid-measurement

The first version gated every window on **20 checkable fixes** — fixes the flow
field had a lane for. That silently deleted exactly the windows the off-road
signal exists to judge: a vessel out where the fleet does not go produces almost
no checkable fixes *because* she is out there. On dev at W=12 h it cut positives
from 17 to 8 and then reported the survivors as the population.

Support is now asked of the **feature**, not the window (`Window.support`):
`off_road_fraction` rests on on-road plus off-road fixes, the surprise
percentiles on checkable fixes, the kinematic features on all fixes. The window
floor is now `n_scored ≥ 20` — did she move enough to be asked anything.

This changed measured numbers, not just code, so it is recorded rather than
quietly corrected. Best dev single-feature precision went 0.111 → 0.174.

## 4. Results on dev

### 4.1 The confound, and its removal

**Before — per hull, fit+dev, 460 hulls, 37 anomalous** (AUC, Mann-Whitney;
values below 0.5 mean *smaller is more anomalous*):

| quantity | AUC | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `n_checkable` | 0.116 | **0.884 inverted** — the strongest signal in the table |
| `span_hours` | 0.117 | **0.883 inverted** |
| `longest_gap_hours` | 0.138 | 0.862 inverted |
| `n_scored` | 0.142 | 0.858 inverted |
| `straightness` | 0.839 | best *behavioural* feature — and see below |
| `course_sigma_p50` | 0.774 | |
| `off_road_fraction` | 0.765 | |

Anomalous hulls: span p50 **15.0 h**, 181 positions. Ordinary: **149.2 h**, 1,078
positions. Per family: `imagery` 2.0 h, `dark_transfer` 11.0 h,
`behavioural_geographic` 17.5 h, `identity_manipulation` 20.5 h, `paperwork`
77.6 h, `graph_ownership` 120.9 h.

**The mechanism.** The generator mints most scenario vessels *for the duration of
their vignette* — a rendezvous is a pair of hulls that exist for the 2.5–13 hours
of the rendezvous — while the background fleet broadcasts across the whole 51-day
window. "Short track" is therefore a near-perfect read on "this hull was
scripted". It transfers to real AIS as **nothing**, because there every hull
broadcasts continuously.

Note `straightness` too: at 0.839 it looks like the best behavioural feature, but
anomalous hulls score 0.888 against 0.015. A 15-hour track is a straight dash; a
149-hour track wanders the whole coast. It is the same artifact wearing a
behavioural name.

**After — fit+dev, W=12 h, 2,869 windows past the `n_scored` floor, 57 positive:**

| stratified by | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | flat? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `observed_span_hours` | 0.0223 | 0.0181 | 0.0084 | 0.0306 | **yes**, CIs overlap |
| `n_fixes` | 0.0265 | 0.0209 | 0.0153 | 0.0167 | **yes** |
| `offshore_km_median` | 0.0223 | 0.0223 | 0.0139 | 0.0209 | **yes** |
| `check_coverage` | 0.0307 | 0.0209 | 0.0139 | 0.0139 | no — see §4.2 |
| `n_windows_in_hull` | 0.0432 | 0.0363 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | **no** — see §4.3 |

Span ranges 9.65–12.0 h across all four strata, so span is constant by
construction and the residual 20% carries no signal. **The confound named in the
previous session is gone.** The same check on the test split (§5.1) agrees.

### 4.2 `check_coverage` is a confound *and* is disqualified on principle

Low-coverage windows are 2–6× more likely to be positive. It is tempting, and it
is banned. `check_coverage` counts `unwatched` fixes — water where the fitted
fleet gave us no basis to say what normal is — against the hull. Flagging on it
is precisely CLAUDE.md's "asserting intentional silence offshore": a false
positive by construction. `off_road_fraction` is the disciplined cousin and
excludes `unwatched` from its denominator; it was used, and `low_check_coverage`
was refused.

**Refusing it cost nothing.** Measured on test for disclosure (§5.4), the
coverage-derived rule scores 0.037 — no better than the rule that refuses it.

### 4.3 The residual confound that could NOT be removed

`n_windows_in_hull`: positive rate 0.043 / 0.036 / 0.000 / 0.000 across
quartiles. This is the hull-lifetime artifact surviving at the *population*
level — briefly-watched hulls are the scripted ones, so windows drawn from
short-lived hulls carry nearly all the positives, and roughly half the window
population is guaranteed-negative.

It cannot be removed without discarding data, and it is stated rather than
worked around. **A window-level detector cannot read it directly** (it is a
hull-level property, and no window feature exposes it), so it does not hand the
rule a free signal — but it does mean the window population is not
exchangeable, and it inflates any *hull-level* roll-up. Anyone re-running this
should treat it as the reason the corpus, not the method, sets the ceiling.

### 4.4 What dev said

W=12 h, 2,168 dev windows, 1,240 scoreable, **16–17 positive**.

| rule | flagged | TP | precision | recall | lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `stopped_offshore_hours ≥ 3.873` (best single) | 62 | 6 | 0.097 | 0.353 | 12× |
| `stopped_offshore > 0.868 & longest_gap > 1.083` (**chosen**) | 15 | 6 | **0.400** | 0.353 | 51× |
| `longest_gap > 1.083 & low_check_coverage > 0.458` | 11 | 5 | 0.455 | 0.294 | 58× |

At W=6 h the best pair reached 0.500 (12 flagged, 6 TP).

**1,667 pairs were evaluated at W=12 h and 1,737 at W=6 h, against 16 positives.**
That multiplicity is stated here because it is the whole explanation of §5.

The chosen rule reads plainly — *she stopped, far offshore, and went quiet for
over an hour, within the same twelve hours* — which is a genuine dark-transfer
signature, and it avoids every coverage-derived feature. On dev it caught
`behavioural_geographic` only, and no `dark_transfer` at all.

**Dev hull roll-up:** 8 hulls flagged, 3 correct — below the ten-flagged floor,
so noise, and reported as such rather than as 0.375.

### 5.5 The oracle bound — and the disclosure that comes with it

**The test split was opened a second time**, deliberately, by
`scratchpad/w8_oracle.py`. This is disclosed here in the same breath as its
result, per the protocol in §2.

The reported 0.032 leaves one question open that matters more than the number:
is 0.70 out of reach because *threshold selection* failed on 16 dev positives,
or because **no rule of this shape clears it on held-out hulls at all**? Those
have opposite implications — the first says "get more dev positives", the second
says "stop".

So thresholds were chosen **on the test split itself**, over the same feature
set: ~2,035 rules at W=12 h and ~2,066 at W=6 h with ≥10 windows flagged. This is
cheating by construction and the result is **not an achievable operating point**.
It is an upper bound.

| W | best ORACLE-selected rule | flagged | TP | precision | 95% CI | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 h | `off_road_fraction ≥ 0.776 & course_sigma_p50 ≥ 0.373` | 10 | 2 | **0.200** | 0.06–0.51 | 0.077 |
| 6 h | `off_road_fraction ≥ 0.464 & closeness_km ≥ −0.162` | 10 | 2 | **0.200** | 0.06–0.51 | 0.039 |

**The ceiling is 0.20, and it is a real ceiling.** Even granted the answer key at
selection time, across ~4,100 candidate rules and two window lengths, no
motion-only rule on this corpus exceeds precision 0.20 — and it reaches that only
on 10 flagged windows with 2 correct, which is at the noise floor anyway. The
gap to ADR-004's 0.70 is not a tuning gap.

This is what turns §7's recommendation from a judgement into a measurement: the
answer is "stop", not "get more dev positives". Note also that the oracle's best
rules are built from `off_road_fraction` and the course-surprise features — the
principled, coverage-disciplined signals — rather than from the gap-and-coverage
features that won on dev. The dev winners were noise; the oracle at least points
at the right physics, and still cannot clear 0.20.

### 5.6 What the number can and cannot support

With 39 positive windows and 31 flagged, the interval on 0.032 is 0.006–0.162: a
true precision as high as 0.16 cannot be excluded. **0.70 is excluded
decisively.** Nothing here should be read as "the signal is exactly at chance" —
only that it is not distinguishable from it, and is nowhere near the bar.

## 6. What was tried and failed

* **Whole-hull scoring with the ADR-042 feature set.** Reached AUC 0.84 and
  looked like a result. It was track length (§4.1). Abandoned — this is the
  session's main finding, not a footnote.
* **Gating windows on checkable-fix count.** A real defect that deleted half the
  positives before scoring; fixed mid-flight (§3.4).
* **Single motion features on windows.** Best dev precision 0.097–0.174; on test,
  0.008–0.031. Failed.
* **~3,400 two-feature conjunctions.** Best dev 0.40–0.50; on test 0.032. Failed,
  and the failure is instructive: this is what fitting 16 positives looks like.
* **Vessel-to-vessel rendezvous features** (`rdv_hours`, `closeness_km`,
  `n_near_hulls`, computed per 30-minute slot so they attribute to a window
  rather than a hull). Built specifically because `dark_transfer` is a
  rendezvous and the chosen rule caught none. They did **not** appear in any
  competitive dev rule. Worth re-testing on a corpus with more than 15 scored
  `dark_transfer` hulls; not evidence against the idea, evidence that this
  corpus cannot test it.
* **Hull-level roll-up.** Below the reporting floor on dev; 0.077 on test. Its
  own bias runs the other way (long-watched hulls get more chances), so it is
  not a fix for the confound and is not offered as one.
* **The oracle sweep** (§5.5) — ~4,100 rules with thresholds chosen on test.
  Max precision 0.200. This "failed" in the most useful way available: it
  converts "we could not find a good rule" into "there is no good rule of this
  shape to find", which is the difference between an unfinished search and a
  finished answer.
* **Window lengths 6 / 12 / 24 h.** All measured. 6 h yields the most windows and
  the fewest positives per window; 24 h leaves 6 dev positives, too few to tune
  on. 12 h was chosen on dev as the least-bad, and none of the three would have
  changed the conclusion.

## 7. Recommended operating point, and whether to promote

**Recommendation: do not promote. There is no operating point to recommend.**

The novelty score should **not** become a registered suspicion factor. On the
untouched test split it does not select — precision 0.032 at lift 2.0× — and
ADR-004's ≥0.70 policy exists precisely to keep signals like this away from an
analyst. Shipping it would spend the operator's attention on a coin flip and
spend the product's credibility on it twice.

`test_projection_is_not_a_registered_suspicion_factor` guards the old residual
signal and **stays exactly as it is**. No new rule is registered, so no new guard
is needed; if a later session promotes a windowed novelty rule, that guard's
sibling should be written at the same time.

What *is* worth keeping is the machinery, and it is checked in and tested:

* the window as the unit of evidence — **this should be the default for every
  future per-vessel precision claim on this corpus**, whatever the signal;
* `span_flatness` as a mandatory pre-flight on any such claim;
* `hull_anomaly_intervals` — time-aware labels;
* the per-feature support model.

**What would have to change for a different answer.** Two things, and the oracle
bound (§5.5) is what separates them.

*Statistical power* is one binding constraint: a split holds 16–39 positive
windows, so selecting among thousands of rules against them overfits by
construction — that is the whole story of dev 0.40 → test 0.032. Fixing it means
injecting far more motion-expressed anomalies, targeting ≳200 positive windows
per split.

But power alone is **not** the ceiling, and this is the more important half.
Given the answer key at selection time, the best motion-only rule still reaches
only **0.200**. So even a corpus with ten times the positives would not carry
this signal to 0.70; it would only let us measure 0.20 precisely. **Motion-only
novelty is not the lever.** The precision budget belongs with the rule modules
that already work on declared data (`anomaly/identity.py`, `voyage.py`,
`paperwork.py`), whose signals are three-valued and do not have to out-compete a
1% base rate — or with a signal that fuses motion with declared identity rather
than replacing it.

**Protocol disclosures, in full:**

* **Test was opened twice, not once.** The first opening
  (`w6_TEST.py`) produced the reported result in §5.2 and was the only one that
  informed any choice — no threshold, feature or rule was changed after it. The
  second (`w8_oracle.py`, §5.5) selected thresholds *on test on purpose*, to
  establish the oracle ceiling of 0.20; nothing was tuned from it and no rule is
  proposed off it. The disclosure is made here rather than buried because a
  reader who assumed a single opening would over-trust the 0.032's interval —
  the honest reading is that both numbers come from the same 39 positives.
* The 40/30/30 split, seed 7, was fixed in §2 before any number was measured and
  was not changed afterwards — including when it became clear that a larger dev
  split would have had more positives to tune on.
* Dev was looked at freely, as the protocol allows; ~3,400 rules were evaluated
  on it, and that is why the dev figures are not results.
* Every figure is **on the synthetic corpus**, from one deterministic route
  generator, and a route-fitted model is flattered by construction. No dark
  vessel has been detected on real data.

## 8. Proposed ADR text — for the parent to paste

> ### ADR-043 — Per-vessel precision is measured on fixed-length windows, and motion novelty is not promoted
>
> **Status:** accepted
> **Supersedes nothing. Extends ADR-042.**
>
> **Context.** ADR-042 measured route-aware forward projection at chance and
> refused to promote it. A successor signal — a *novelty* score asking "how
> improbable is what she is doing, given what traffic does here" rather than
> "is she where we predicted" — was built and measured against the same
> policy bar, ADR-004's ≥0.70.
>
> Measuring it per hull over whole tracks first produced an apparent AUC of
> 0.84. That number was an artifact. In the synthetic corpus, anomalous hulls
> are watched for a median of 15 hours and ordinary hulls for 149, because the
> generator mints most scenario vessels for the duration of their vignette
> while the background fleet runs the entire corpus window. Track length alone
> separates the two classes with AUC 0.88 — better than any behavioural
> feature. Every feature correlates with observation length, so every feature
> looked excellent, and what was being measured was which vessels the generator
> had scripted. In real AIS every hull broadcasts continuously, so that
> separation transfers as nothing.
>
> **Decision.**
>
> 1. **The unit of any per-vessel precision claim on this corpus is a
>    fixed-length window** (12 h, tiled per track, kept only when fixes span
>    ≥80% of it), never a whole track. This removes observation length by
>    construction and reverses the residual bias into the safe direction: a
>    long-watched hull gets more chances to look odd, not a longer look.
> 2. **A precision claim must be preceded by a published flatness check**
>    (`prediction_eval.span_flatness`) showing the positive rate is flat across
>    quartiles of window span, fix count, and any other quantity suspected of
>    carrying the separation. A claim without it is not admissible.
> 3. **Window labels are time-scoped**, from `scenario_truth`'s `t_start`/`t_end`
>    (`scenario.measure.hull_anomaly_intervals`). A hull is a positive only
>    while her scenario is running.
> 4. **Feature support is per feature, not per window.** Gating a window on
>    the count of flow-field-checkable fixes deletes exactly the off-road
>    windows the off-road signal exists to judge.
> 5. **`check_coverage` and anything derived from it may not be used as a
>    suspicion feature.** It counts `unwatched` water against a hull, which is
>    CLAUDE.md's "asserting intentional silence offshore" — a false positive by
>    construction. Refusing it was measured to cost nothing.
> 6. **Motion novelty is NOT promoted to a suspicion factor.** On the untouched
>    test split (199 hulls, 2,396 windows, 39 positive) the best dev-chosen rule
>    scores precision **0.032** (31 flagged, 1 correct, 95% CI 0.006–0.162,
>    recall 0.026, lift 2.0×). Its dev figure of 0.40 was the best of ~1,700
>    candidate rules fitted against 16 positive windows and did not survive
>    held-out hulls.
> 7. **The ceiling is recorded as 0.20, measured rather than estimated.** With
>    thresholds chosen on the test split itself — cheating deliberately, across
>    ~4,100 candidate rules and two window lengths — the best precision any
>    motion-only rule reaches is 0.200, on 10 flagged windows. The gap to 0.70
>    is therefore structural and not a tuning gap. This required a second,
>    disclosed opening of the test split, which informed no choice.
>
> **Consequences.** The second attempt at a motion-only suspicion signal is
> refused on measurement, as the first was — and this time the refusal is
> stronger than "we could not tune it": an oracle that is *given* the answer key
> at selection time still cannot clear 0.20. The ceiling is set by the corpus and
> by the signal, not by the search. Raising it requires a corpus with roughly an
> order of magnitude more motion-expressed anomalies (≳200 positive windows per
> split) *and* a signal with more separation than motion alone provides; until
> then the precision budget belongs with the declared-data rule modules, whose
> signals do not have to out-compete a 1% base rate.
>
> The window machinery, the flatness check, the time-scoped labels and the
> support model are retained and tested, and are now the required shape of any
> future per-vessel precision measurement.
>
> **Every figure in this ADR is on the synthetic corpus**, generated by one
> deterministic route generator (`scenario/searoute.py`), and must be
> re-measured on the deploy host before any of it is stated externally
> (CLAUDE.md §4.6, §5). No dark vessel has been detected on real data.

## 9. Reproducing this

**The reusable parts are in the repo and tested** — `tracks/novelty.py`
(windows, rules, verdicts), `tracks/prediction_eval.py` (`TruthIntervals`,
`score_windows`, `sweep_window_threshold`, `hull_level_from_windows`,
`span_flatness`) and `scenario/measure.hull_anomaly_intervals`. Anyone redoing
this measurement should call those rather than the scripts below.

The scripts below are **scratchpad harness only**, and the scratchpad is
session-scoped — treat this table as a record of the order the measurement ran
in, not as files that will still be there. Re-creating them from this table plus
the repo functions is a couple of hours' work; none of them writes to the repo.

|---|---|
| `p1_build.py` | builds 662 tracks from the landed corpus → `tracks8.pkl` |

| `w1_windows.py` | cuts windows at 6/12/24 h → `windows8.pkl`; first confound check |
